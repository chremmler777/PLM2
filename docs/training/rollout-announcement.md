**Subject:** Engineering changes move into PLM2 on [go-live date]: your role and your training

Hi everyone,

From [go-live date] every engineering change request runs in PLM2, from the
customer's request to the released part. One record per change, the same
information for everyone:

**https://apps.ad.us.ktx.group/plm2/**

Open it in the browser and sign in with your KTX account. Nothing to install.

## What we get out of it

- **One place.** The request, the assessments, the costs, the offer, the
  plan and the release are on one change page. No more hunting through mails
  and spreadsheets.
- **One owner per step.** Every step names who acts next. The page says what
  it is waiting for under "Blocked by".
- **Nothing gets lost.** Every answer, every date move and every decision
  carries a name and a time.
- **Offer versus reality.** Each change compares what we offered with what it
  really cost and how long it really took. That comparison is how we get
  better at quoting.

## How a change travels

| Step | Who acts |
|---|---|
| Captured | Sales starts the request (Project Management may too) |
| Scoping | Project Management with the team; Development locks what is impacted |
| Assessment | The routed engineering departments answer the checklist and give a verdict |
| Costing | The departments enter their costs and lead times; Project Management closes costing |
| Offer and negotiation | Sales builds and sends the offer, logs every round, records the customer's answer |
| Approved | Project Management and Quality sign off; the team confirms the detailed plan |
| Implementation | Each department updates its own blocks in the plan; date moves need a reason |
| Validation and release | Each department ticks its release checklist items; a failed check becomes a validation issue |
| Closed | Project Management closes the change after the summary |

Three special cases have their own short path, explained in the manual:
changes from KTX Weissenburg or KTX Solingen (no assessment and no offer, we
start with bank-build planning), new customer indexes (Development decides
how deep each one goes), and validation issues (a failed check gets a
recovery plan and, if needed, an escalation).

## Project team: one responsible per role

On each project one person per department is the responsible. Their tasks
count on their badge and their notifications. Everyone else in the
department is backup: they see the same tasks, marked "Backup", and can act
on them. If you are backup and you act, the record says you acted for the
responsible.

## Everybody takes the training

Every role has a short chapter, a one-page handout and a practical check of
three or four tasks. The check runs on the real screens in a training copy:
nothing you do there touches a real change. It takes about ten minutes.

1. Come to the session for your role: [session dates].
2. Afterwards, open **Training** in the sidebar and press "Confirm your
   training". Name your trainer and the day of the session.
3. Do the practical check. Retry as often as you like. Every attempt is
   recorded, and only your pass counts.

**Training is recorded, not blocking.** You can work on changes before you
have finished it. The record is for the audit: who was trained, by whom, on
which version of the software. When a new version of the material is
published, your record shows re-training as due and only the practical check
is taken again.

The manual stays under **Training**, "Manual". It is the same material,
there whenever you need to look something up.

## What managers and department leads are responsible for

- **Check your department is complete.** Go through your people now and say
  who is missing, who has left, and who sits in the wrong department. A
  person in the wrong department sees the wrong tasks.
- **Name the responsible per project.** The Project Manager sets the project
  team on the project page. Tell them who leads for your department on which
  project.
- **Keep your people trained.** New starters and transfers take the session
  and the check before they work on a change.

## If something looks wrong, say so

The ECR screens are new and will have rough edges. If something behaves
oddly, looks wrong or gets in your way, tell us instead of working around it.
Include the change number, what you were doing, and the time it happened.
